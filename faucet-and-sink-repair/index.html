Diagnosing The Problem

What Causes Most Faucet Problems

A dripping faucet almost always traces to a worn washer, O-ring, or cartridge inside the valve body - which part depends on the faucet's specific design, and using the wrong replacement part is a common reason a DIY fix doesn't actually stop the drip. Low water pressure at one fixture specifically, rather than the whole house, often points to a partially closed shutoff valve or mineral buildup inside the aerator or the valve itself, not a house-wide pressure problem.

A faucet or valve that's fully stuck, especially on an older fixture, is frequently corrosion at the valve stem - forcing it can shear the stem off entirely, which turns a simple repair into a full fixture replacement, so it's worth having it looked at before that happens.

Connections After An Appliance Install

We regularly get calls to reconnect a water line to a dishwasher or refrigerator after a kitchen remodel or appliance swap, or to address a garbage disposal that's leaking water from its connection to the sink. These are usually straightforward, same-visit fixes once we're looking at the actual setup.

When Repair Doesn't Make Sense Anymore

A very old faucet with mineral-clogged internals, multiple prior repairs, or parts that are no longer manufactured is sometimes better replaced than repaired again - we'll tell you honestly when that's the case rather than patching something that's going to fail again in a few months. Every repair and installation follows California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) standards for supply line material and shutoff-valve access.

Outdoor Faucets And Hose Bibs

Outdoor hose bibs take a different kind of wear than indoor fixtures - freeze damage is less of a concern in this climate than in colder regions, but UV exposure, mineral buildup, and simple age still cause the same drips and stuck handles over time. A leaking or frozen-open hose bib left unaddressed can waste a meaningful amount of water and, on an older line, sometimes points to a supply line issue further back that's worth checking while we're already there.

Choosing A Replacement Fixture

If you're replacing rather than repairing, fixture quality varies more than people expect - a cheap faucet with plastic internals often needs its cartridge replaced again within a couple of years, while a mid-range fixture with a ceramic disc valve typically lasts far longer with the same use. We're happy to talk through what's worth spending more on and where it doesn't matter much, rather than just installing whatever you hand us without comment.

Bathroom Sink Stoppers And Drain Assemblies

A bathroom sink that won't hold water, or a stopper that's stuck open or closed, is usually a worn or misaligned pop-up assembly rather than anything wrong with the drain itself - a quick adjustment or a replacement linkage typically solves it in one visit, and it's often bundled with a faucet repair call at no significant added cost.

Shutoff Valves Worth Checking

The individual shutoff valves under a sink - one for hot, one for cold - sometimes seize up from age and mineral deposits even when the faucet itself is working fine, which becomes a real problem the day you actually need to shut water off quickly for a repair. If yours haven't been operated in years, having them tested and replaced if needed during an otherwise unrelated visit is a cheap way to avoid a bigger headache later.

Common Questions

Quick Answers

Can you match the exact faucet brand I have?

In most cases yes - bring us the brand and model if you know it, or we can identify it on-site before ordering parts.

Is a dripping faucet actually costing me money?

Yes - a faucet dripping once per second can waste over 3,000 gallons a year, which shows up on your water bill even though it seems minor.

Do you install fixtures we've already purchased?

Yes, we install customer-supplied faucets and fixtures as long as they're compatible with the existing plumbing.
