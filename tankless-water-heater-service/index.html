What You Need To Know

Why Tankless Units Need Service That Tank Heaters Don't

A tankless water heater's heat exchanger runs mineral-heavy water through narrow passages every time hot water is drawn. In much of Conejo Valley, tap water carries enough hardness that scale buildup inside that exchanger is normal wear, not a defect - and it's the single most common reason a tankless unit starts delivering lukewarm water, throws an error code, or shows reduced flow a few years in. A descale flush, using a pump and a vinegar or manufacturer-approved descaling solution circulated through the unit per the manufacturer's service guidelines, clears that buildup and is genuinely the fix in most cases we see, not a full replacement.

We hear a version of the same call often: someone who bought a tankless unit expecting to never think about it again, then got an error code out of nowhere. Most of the time the fix is a flush, not a new unit, and we'll tell you that honestly rather than upselling a replacement you don't need yet.

What We Check On A Service Call

A service visit includes reading and explaining any error code the unit is displaying, checking inlet water pressure and flow rate, inspecting the venting for blockage, and performing a full descale flush if it's due (typically annually, more often with harder water or heavy household use). If the issue turns out to be a failed component - an igniter, a flow sensor, a circuit board - we'll price the repair against a straight replacement so you can compare honestly rather than defaulting to whichever is more profitable for us.

New Installs

If you're installing a tankless unit for the first time, sizing and venting matter more than with a tank system - an undersized unit will bottleneck simultaneous hot water draws, and venting has to meet the manufacturer's exact spec, not just a general code minimum. We size to your actual household and handle the full install, including any gas line or electrical work the new unit requires.

Extending The Life Of A Tankless Unit

Beyond annual descaling, a tankless unit lasts longest when the inlet water filter (most units have one) is checked periodically and when the unit isn't asked to run well past its rated flow rate for the fixture count it's serving. If you've added bathrooms or fixtures since the unit was installed, that's worth mentioning when you call - a unit that was correctly sized for a 2-bath house can genuinely struggle once a third bathroom or a large soaking tub gets added.

Warranty Considerations

Most manufacturer warranties on tankless units require documented annual maintenance to stay valid, and a skipped flush is one of the more common reasons a warranty claim gets denied down the line. We provide a dated service record after every visit specifically so you have that documentation if you ever need it.

Retrofitting An Older Home

Installing a tankless unit in a home that was built around a tank system sometimes means upsizing the gas line, adding a dedicated electrical circuit for the ignition system, or running new venting through an exterior wall - none of that is unusual, but it does change the cost and timeline compared to a straight swap. We assess the existing infrastructure during the quote, not after we've already started, so there's no surprise mid-install change order.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a tankless unit be flushed?

Most manufacturers recommend annual descaling; homes on harder water or heavy daily use may need it more often.

What does an error code usually mean?

It depends on the code and brand, but scale buildup, venting blockage, and low inlet flow are the most common causes we see - we'll read the specific code on-site before quoting anything.

Can you service a brand you didn't install?

Yes, we service all major tankless brands regardless of who installed the unit.
