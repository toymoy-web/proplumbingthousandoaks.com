What You Need To Know

Built Around How Commercial Properties Actually Operate

A leaking fixture in a tenant's unit or a slow drain in a retail bathroom isn't just a plumbing problem - it's a scheduling problem, a tenant-communication problem, and sometimes a liability problem. We work directly with property managers on a work-order basis, coordinate access with tenants ourselves when asked, and schedule around business hours or after-close windows when a repair would otherwise disrupt operations.

We handle everything from a single leaking unit in a multi-tenant property to building-wide issues like a commercial water heater or a grease trap backup. If you're calling on behalf of tenants rather than for yourself, tell us that upfront and we'll manage communication accordingly.

Commercial Leak Detection

Commercial spaces - especially properties with a kitchen, multiple bathrooms, or a grease trap - often have leaks that are harder to isolate than a typical residential call: a slow drain-line leak inside a wall cavity, a grease trap that needs the grate cleared, or water loss that only shows up on the utility bill. We bring the same diagnostic equipment we use on residential slab leaks - acoustic listening and pressure testing - to commercial jobs so we're finding the actual source before opening anything up.

What We Cover

Grease trap service and clearing performed to standard wastewater discharge guidelines, commercial drain and sewer line cleaning, commercial water heater repair and replacement, fixture repair across multi-unit properties, and general maintenance plumbing for offices and retail. If it's outside our scope - large-scale new construction plumbing, for example - we'll tell you that directly instead of taking the job and figuring it out.

Scheduling Around Your Business

A retail space or office doesn't always have the option of shutting down for a repair during business hours. We'll work with you to schedule non-emergency work before open, after close, or during a slower window when that matters more than getting to it the same afternoon - and we'll still treat an actual emergency (active leak, sewage backup, no water to a functioning restroom) with the same urgency we'd give a residential call.

Recurring Maintenance Accounts

Some properties are better served by a standing maintenance relationship than a call-only arrangement - a quarterly grease trap check for a restaurant, or a semi-annual inspection of common-area fixtures across a multi-unit building, catches small issues before they become work orders. If you manage more than one Thousand Oaks property, ask about setting up that kind of recurring service instead of starting from scratch on every call.

Documentation For Owners And Boards

HOA boards and out-of-state owners often need more paperwork than an individual homeowner would ask for - photos, an itemized invoice, sometimes a written scope before work even starts. We build that documentation into the job by default on commercial and multi-unit accounts rather than treating it as a special request, since it tends to save everyone a round of follow-up calls later.

Multi-Unit And Mixed-Use Buildings

A mixed-use building with retail on the ground floor and residential units above adds its own wrinkle - a plumbing issue in one unit can trace back to a shared stack or main line serving several tenants, which means the fix and the responsible party aren't always obvious from a single work order. We diagnose at the building level when that's what the problem actually calls for, and we'll tell you plainly when an issue looks like it's shared infrastructure rather than a single tenant's fixture, so the cost and the repair get attributed correctly.
